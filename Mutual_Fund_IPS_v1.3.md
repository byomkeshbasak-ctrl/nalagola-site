## Part B: Allocation

### B.0 Shares and funds together

New money is split between the share book and this book against a **fixed target of 30% shares and 70% funds** of household equity (band 25–35% for shares). This replaces the proportional split in Stocks IPS v5.2 Part 3.2 through Amendment 1.

For a month's surplus S, with shares worth A and funds worth B:

`To shares = the lower of S and the larger of 0 and (30% × (A + B + S) − A)`; the rest goes to funds.

Direct shares are never allowed above 40% of household equity (v5.2 H1). Nothing is sold to move money between the two books.

The fund book carries the mid and small-cap exposure that a 12% plan needs at maximum risk. Moving towards more direct shares would add concentration without evidence that it pays. ## Part E: New funds and challengers

1. A fund not held becomes a **candidate** when it is in the top quartile of its slot and at least 10 points above the slot fund's score.
2. It becomes a **qualified challenger** after two screens in a row and the Part D.3 checks.
3. **A challenger is bought only when the switch is approved at the same review**: the slot fund meets Part G.2 and its exit is entered in the decision log that day. A challenger never receives money while the old fund is still the slot fund.
4. **First purchase at least 3% of the book.** Smaller amounts wait for a later review.
5. **Passive rule (kept from v1.2 E.4):** an active slot fund that beat its slot index fund in fewer than 50% of rolling 3-year windows at two quarterly checks in a row loses the slot to the index fund, under the same switch rule.
6. One switch per quarter at most.

## Part F: Deploying money

### F.1 Where it comes from

- **Monthly surplus.** The funds' share of last month's cash surplus (Part B.0; definition in Stocks IPS v5.2 Part 3.1), invested at the **1st review of the next month**. A negative month is skipped with no carry-forward.
- **Sale proceeds** from this book, at the same review as the sale.

### F.2 Algorithm

1. **Hard limits first** (Part B.2), then category limits, then bucket bands, then slot shares.
2. **Bucket:** the bucket furthest below its band; if none is below its band, the one with the largest rupee gap to target. A bucket at or above target gets nothing. If international can't be bought, Part B.4 resets the targets first.
3. **Slot:** within the bucket, the slot furthest below its share.
4. **Fund:** the slot fund. Never a fund marked exit, frozen or losing its pair. While a pair is undecided (until 1 Jan 2027), the bucket's money goes to its single-fund slots first; a paired slot receives nothing.
5. **Amount:** the least of the gap to the slot share, the room under the ceiling, the room under the at-cost limit, the room under the category limit and the money left.
6. **All at once.** Each amount is invested in one purchase at the review. The 200-day-average pacing rule and the 4–8 week tranches are dropped.
7. **Remainder:** if nothing can take the rest, it waits in the broker account for the next review.

## Part G: Selling

### G.1 Calendar

Fund sales follow the share book (Stocks IPS v5.2 Part 6.1):
- **Long-term units** (domestic funds: held more than 12 months; international FoFs: more than 24 months) may be sold at **any 1st or 15th review**.
- **Short-term units** are sold only for a quality break (G.2) or an emergency (G.6).

### G.2 Quality break: exit in full

Mandate drift, an AMC governance event, a manager change that removes the reason for owning the fund, or Part D.4. Exit at the next review, short-term units included.

### G.3 Switch to a qualified challenger

At a review where all of these hold:
- the slot fund has been in the bottom half of its slot at two quarterly checks in a row;
- estimated tax plus exit load is below 2% of the amount moved; otherwise the slot fund stays and nothing is bought;
- no other switch has happened in the quarter.

Units still short-term are sold as they turn long-term; until then the old fund gets no money.

### G.4 Settling a pair

The fund that loses a pair (1 Jan 2027) gets no new money from that day. Its long-term units are sold under the tax rules in Part H. Short-term units are sold as they turn long-term, and the proceeds go to the slot fund up to its share.

### G.5 Cap breach

Above a ceiling: no new money, and the excess is trimmed from long-term units at the next review the tax rules in Part H allow.

### G.6 Emergency selling order

When the household needs cash and the fund book is the source:
1. Sell from the **bucket furthest above its target** (in percentage points) first, then the next.
2. Within a bucket, sell the fund furthest above its slot share first.
3. Units leave first-in, first-out, as the rules require.
4. Record the sale, its reason and the tax in the decision log. The next surplus refills the bucket through Part F.
