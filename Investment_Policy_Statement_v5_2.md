**Status:** APPROVED by the investor on 20 Nov 2026. Operative.

> This is one investor's rulebook for his own decisions. It is not personalised advice. Returns are targets, not promises, and capital can be lost. Tax figures are estimates until a chartered accountant or the broker's tax P&L confirms them.

### Why v5.2 exists

v5.1 was written for a book that no longer exists. It assumed eight stocks and ₹20 L, carried about forty rules that need data the tracker does not have, and never tied the stock book to the household goal. Since then the October sale took out BEL, Naukri, NIFTYBEES and the Zerodha IOC shares, and the ₹6,05,156 it raised went into the flat's interiors. v5.2 does five things:

1. States the goal as a number with a return the plan can actually lean on (Part 1).
2. Rebases every limit and plan to the five stocks held on 20 Nov 2026 (Part 2).
3. Adds the monthly surplus rule: last month's cash surplus is invested every month, split between stocks and funds in proportion to their current values (Part 3).
4. Replaces the quarterly-only trim calendar and the CA-dependent tax control with a lowest-tax plan that runs at every 1st and 15th review (Parts 6 and 7).
5. Moves every rule that needs unavailable data to a parked list (Part 11), so nothing in force reports UNVERIFIED forever.

The full change list against v5.1 is in Appendix A.

### 1.2 Is 20% a year feasible?

The investor's expectation is 20% a year on current investments. That is not a sound number to plan on, for three reasons:

- The broad Indian market (Nifty 500 TRI) has compounded at roughly 12–14% a year over long periods. A 20% compound rate for fourteen years would put this book ahead of almost every professional fund in India over that span.
- The stock book today is 69% two private banks. Banks of that size have rarely compounded at 20% for a decade or more.
- A plan that needs 20% fails quietly. Each year at 14% looks fine until the shortfall is too large to make up in the last few years.

## Part 3: New money and the household split

### 3.1 The monthly surplus

**Definition.** For each calendar month M:

`Surplus(M) = income received in M − expenses paid in M from bank accounts or cash − credit card bill payments made in M`

- Income: salary, interest, refunds, any other income entered in the tracker, **plus the wine shop's net profit for M** as saved by the shop accounts page.
- Expenses include every EMI (home loan, car loan and any card EMI) and one-off spends such as interiors.
- Spends charged to a credit card are counted once, when the card bill is paid. An expense entered against a card is therefore not deducted a second time.
- Market gains, EPF additions and changes in shop capital are not income for this purpose.

**Rules.**

1. The surplus for M is invested at the **1st review of month M+1** (the review on the 1st).
2. If Surplus(M) is zero or negative, nothing is invested that month. A negative month is not carried forward and does not reduce later months.
3. There is no emergency-fund or EMI top-up before investing. The investor has chosen this; Part 9 records the risk.
4. The money is moved from the bank to the broker before the review, using the tracker's transfer form, so the trade can settle.

November 2026 is negative because of the ₹4 L interiors payment, so nothing is invested on 1 Dec 2026.

### 3.2 Split between stocks and funds

New money is split **in proportion to the current market values** of the two books on the day of the review:

`Stocks share = stock book value ÷ (stock book value + mutual fund value)`

At the baseline this is 30.7% to stocks and 69.3% to funds. Because new money follows the existing mix, the mix drifts only with returns. Nothing is sold to rebalance between the two books.

One override applies (Part 8.2): if direct stocks exceed **40% of HE**, the stock share of new money is nil until they are back below 40%.

**Why proportional and not a fixed 40/60.** v4.9 aimed for 40% in direct stocks. There is no evidence yet that this book's stock selection beats the market, and its funds give broader exposure, including mid and small caps that the stock screen excludes. Moving towards more stocks would raise concentration risk without a record that it pays. The stock share can be raised after the first three-year check in Part 8 if the stock book has earned it.

### 3.3 Where the money goes inside each book

- **Stocks share:** to the buy list from the Stocks review (Part 5), in the order the engine ranks it: first names below target, then new names. Never to a name above its target or to an industry at its limit. If the buy list cannot use the full amount, the rest buys a Nifty 500 index fund or ETF (Part 8).
- **Funds share:** to the buy list of the Mutual Fund IPS v1.2 review on the same day.
- Amounts below ₹5,000 for one name are rolled into the next name on the list rather than bought as tiny lots.

## Part 5: Selecting and buying stocks

### 5.1 Universe and quality gate (carried from v4.9)

Universe: the Nifty LargeMidcap 250, refreshed by the `stock-fundamentals` screen. A stock may be bought only if it passes the quality gate on the screen's latest data:

| Test | Non-financial companies | Banks and NBFCs |
|---|---|---|
| Return on capital | ROCE ≥ 15% (3-year average) | ROE ≥ 15% (3-year average) |
| Floor | No year below 10% in the last three | Same |
| Debt | Debt/equity ≤ 2.0 | Not tested |
| Cash conversion | Operating cash flow ≥ 70% of net profit (3-year) | Not tested |
| Valuation | P/E not more than 2 standard deviations above its own 5-year average | P/B on the same test, checked by hand until the feed has P/B history |

A held name that fails the gate gets no new money and goes on probation (Part 6.4).

### 5.2 Ranking

Eligible stocks are scored on quality (40%), growth (30%), valuation (15%) and balance sheet (15%) within their group (financials and non-financials ranked separately), as v4.9 set. No more than two names from one business group (HDFC, ICICI, Tata, Bajaj and so on) are held.

### 5.3 How a purchase is made

| Rule | Value |
|---|---|
| First purchase in a new name | Up to half its target weight |
| Later purchases | At least 28 days after the previous one, up to 2.5% of the stock book each |
| Quarterly limit per name | 5% of the stock book |
| Ceiling | Never above the name's target weight |
| Order type | Limit order within 0.5% of the review price |

The amount bought is the smallest of: the tranche allowance, the gap to target, the gap to the hard cap, the quarterly allowance and the money available.

## Part 6: Selling, trimming and the concentration plan

### 6.1 When reviews happen

Reviews run on the **1st and 15th of every month**. Every review may buy. From v5.2:

- **Sales of long-term lots** (held more than 12 months) may happen at **any** 1st or 15th review.
- **Sales of short-term lots** happen only for a Tier 1 or Tier 2 exit (Part 6.4). A trim never sells a short-term lot.

This replaces the v5.1 rule that held every sale for a quarterly action review.

### 6.2 IOC exit (DL-001, confirmed)

The remaining 2,000 IOC shares in Angel One are sold in full at the **1 Dec 2026 review**. All lots are long-term; the estimated gain is ₹39,740 (cost ₹2,61,260).

Waiting until April 2027 would put that gain into next year's tax-free allowance and save about ₹5,000. The investor has chosen to exit now, and the cost is small next to keeping 19% of the book in a cyclical refiner.

### 6.3 Concentration plan

The plan clears the breaches in Part 2.4 in this order, always selling the oldest lot first and never a short-term lot:

| Review | Action | Why then |
|---|---|---|
| **1 Dec 2026** | Sell IOC in full. Sell HDFC's 52 oldest shares, **only if** the broker's tax P&L confirms they carry a long-term loss | The HDFC loss of about ₹35,000 offsets most of the IOC gain, and banks fall by ₹44,000 |
| 1 Dec 2026 onward | IOC and HDFC proceeds (about ₹3.45 L) buy the Part 5 list: new names first, at half-target first tranches | Brings the name count from 4 to 8 or more by Jan 2027 |
| **1 Apr 2027** | Trim HDFC to 20% of the stock book from the 200 bonus shares | First review of FY 2027-28; the gain falls inside the new ₹1.25 L allowance |
| **15 Jun 2027** | Trim ICICI to 20% of the stock book | ICICI's lot turns long-term on 4 Jun 2027. Any gain above the remaining allowance is taxed at 12.5%, and that is accepted |
| From Jul 2027 | Bring every name to the 10% cap and banks to the 25% industry cap using new money, plus trims sized to the unused allowance each year (Part 7.3) | Lowest tax over time |

**General rule behind the plan:**

1. A name **above 20%** of the stock book is extreme. Its long-term lots are trimmed to 20% at the first review where a long-term lot exists, even if LTCG must be paid. One exception: when this year's allowance is already used and 1 April is less than four months away, the trim waits for the first review of April.
2. A name **between 10% and 20%** is trimmed only by as much gain as fits in the year's unused allowance. Otherwise new money does the work.
3. A name in the warning zone (9–10%) gets no new money. Nothing is sold.

### 6.4 Exit tiers

| Tier | Trigger | Action | Can tax delay it? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Fraud or misstatement found by a regulator, auditor or court; a SEBI, RBI, IRDAI or exchange ban; auditor resigning with concerns or a qualified opinion; insolvency admitted; any promoter pledge | Full exit at the next trading session, short-term lots included | No |
| 2 | A written finding that the business's economics have changed for good (lost licence, structural loss of market, obsolescence) | Full exit within 30 days | Only within the 30 days |
| 3 | The thesis's own written sell condition is met | Full exit within 60 days | Only within the 60 days |
| 4 | Fails the quality gate on the screen's data | No new money; probation for two quarters; exit at the third quarterly check if not cured | Yes, within probation |
| 5 | P/E (or P/B) more than 3 standard deviations above its own 10-year average | Trim 25% at the next review, long-term lots only | Yes |
| 6 | Concentration (Part 6.3) | As Part 6.3 | As Part 6.3 |

A Tier 1 event overrides every other rule in this document.

## Part 7: Tax policy

### 7.1 Rates assumed

Listed equity shares and equity funds, on the rates in force since 23 Jul 2024, to be checked against the Income-tax Act 2025 rules for each year:

| Gain | Holding | Tax |
|---|---|---|
| Short-term | 12 months or less | 20% |
| Long-term | More than 12 months | 12.5% on gains above ₹1.25 L a financial year (stocks and equity funds together) |

Plus surcharge and 4% cess where they apply.

### 7.2 Where FY 2026-27 stands

Sales between Apr and Nov 2026 realised large gains: roughly ₹7 L on shares alone on an average-cost estimate, before short-term losses on CLEAN, V-Guard and Bikaji and before fund gains. **The ₹1.25 L allowance for FY 2026-27 is used up.** Any further long-term gain this year is taxed at 12.5%.

Tax on these gains has not been calculated. Advance tax is due on capital gains in the instalment after the gain arises; the next dates are **15 Dec 2026** and **15 Mar 2027**. Action: get the tax P&L from Zerodha, Angel One and the fund platform, and have the FY 2026-27 liability worked out before 15 Dec 2026.

### 7.3 Lowest-tax rules

1. **No short-term sales by choice.** Short-term lots are sold only for Tier 1 or Tier 2 exits.
2. **Oldest lot first** (the demat rule), so plan each sale on actual lots, not on average cost.
3. **Pair losses with gains.** When a long-term gain must be booked, sell any long-term loss lot in the same financial year (example: the HDFC 2024 lot against the IOC gain).
4. **Use the allowance every year.** At the **1 Mar** review, if less than ₹1.25 L of long-term gain has been booked that financial year, book gains up to the remainder, in this order:
   - long-term lots of names that are above target (a trim that also reduces concentration);
   - otherwise, sell and buy back the same day long-term lots of a fund or stock that stays in the plan. This resets the cost at no tax and lowers future tax.
   Stocks and funds share one allowance; the stock and fund reviews on 1 Mar use it together, stocks first.
5. **Defer discretionary trims across 31 March** when the allowance is used and the new year is under four months away (Part 6.3, rule 1).
6. **Accept the tax** when a name is above 20% of the stock book (Part 6.3) or for any Tier 1 to 3 exit.
7. Each year, by **15 Mar**, the tracker's tax worksheet lists gains by type and the advance tax due. The worksheet replaces v5.1's quarterly CA confirmation, which is parked (Part 11).

## Part 8: Benchmark and the index fallback

Stock picking has to earn its place. Two tests apply together.

### 8.1 Yearly benchmark check

At the **first review of April** each year from April 2027, the tracker compares the stock book's time-weighted return since 1 Dec 2026 with the Nifty 500 TRI over the same period.

| Check | Condition | Result |
|---|---|---|
| April 2027 and April 2028 | Stock book behind the index by more than 3 percentage points a year since 1 Dec 2026 | Warning, recorded in the decision log with a written cause. No change to rules |
| **April 2029 (the three-year check)** | Same condition | **Fallback:** new money for the stock share goes to a Nifty 500 index fund or ETF instead of new names. Existing names are kept, with no new money, and sold only under Part 6 |
| April 2029 | Stock book ahead of or level with the index | The investor may raise the H1 cap in Part 8.2 by amendment |

After a fallback, stock picking can restart only by a written amendment backed by a further three-year record (paper or real) that beats the index.

### 8.2 Cap on the stock book

Direct stocks may not exceed **40% of household equity** (H1). Above that, the stock share of monthly new money is nil and goes to funds instead. Nothing is sold to meet H1. At baseline direct stocks are 30.7%.
